How Calcyworld Works

This page explains what happens between the moment you type a number and the moment you see an answer, so you know how far to trust it.

Calculations happen on your device

The formulas are JavaScript that your browser downloads with the page. When you change an input, your browser recalculates and redraws the result. Your inputs never travel to our server, which is also why results appear instantly and why the tools keep working on a slow connection once the page has loaded.

Rounding

Results are rounded for display only. Inside a calculation, values keep full precision, so a loan schedule adds up to the same total however many decimals you can see. Currency amounts show two decimals, except for currencies that have none, such as the yen or the won. Because of this, a figure you copy from the screen can differ from a bank statement by the smallest unit of the currency.

Exchange rates

Currency rates come from the open data feed of Exchange Rate API and are refreshed twice a day. Crypto prices come from CoinGecko and refresh on the same schedule. These are mid-market rates. A bank, card network or money-transfer service adds a margin, so the amount you receive will usually be a little lower than the amount shown here. Rates are for planning, not for settling a trade.

Units, formats and language

Your country decides the defaults: the currency, how numbers are grouped (1,000,000 or 10,00,000), the date order and whether length and weight start in metric or imperial. Every tool lets you change the unit it uses. The country picker in the header overrides the guess.

Popular in your country

To show which tools are popular where you are, Calcyworld counts page views per tool, per country and per day. It stores no cookie and no IP address for this, and it ignores visitors who have turned on Do Not Track. Until enough views have been counted for a country, a default list is shown.

What a calculator cannot do

A calculator applies a formula to the numbers you give it. It cannot know your lender’s fees, your tax situation, your medical history or the building code of your town. Treat results as a well-organised estimate and check anything important with the lender, tax office, doctor or engineer who is responsible for the decision. More on this is on the Disclaimer page.
