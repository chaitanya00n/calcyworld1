How an EMI is calculated, and why early payments are mostly interest

November 6, 2026 Rezoic

An EMI, or equated monthly instalment, is the fixed amount you pay every month until a loan is cleared. The amount never changes, but what it is made of does: at the start most of it is interest, and at the end most of it is principal.

The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is the amount borrowed, r is the interest rate for one month (the yearly rate divided by 12), and n is the number of monthly payments.

A worked example

Borrow 100,000 at 9% a year for 5 years. Here r = 0.09 ÷ 12 = 0.0075 and n = 60, which gives an EMI of about 2,075.84. Over 60 payments you repay 124,550, so the interest is about 24,550.

In the first month the interest is 750 (100,000 × 0.0075), so only 1,325.84 of your payment reduces the loan. By the last year the interest part has shrunk to roughly 1,170 for the whole year, against about 8,330 in the first year.

What this tells you

  • Paying extra early saves the most, because the balance that interest is charged on falls sooner.
  • A longer term lowers the EMI but raises the total interest sharply. The same 100,000 over 20 years costs about 900 a month, and about 115,900 in interest, almost five times as much.
  • A small rate difference matters more on a long loan than on a short one.
